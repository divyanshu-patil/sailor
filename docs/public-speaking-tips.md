# Public Speaking Tips

36 short, practical tips from well-known speakers and coaches, grouped by when you use them. Each one is a single thing to do, and says who it comes from.

---

## Before you speak

1. **Know your one idea.** If you can't say it in one sentence, the audience won't remember it. — *Chris Anderson (TED)*
2. **Decide what they should know or do at the end.** It's your speech's GPS: every line should move toward it. — *MicFront*
3. **Make the audience the hero.** You're the guide; they're the ones who change. — *Nancy Duarte*
4. **Give them a reason to care.** Start from a question or problem they already have. — *Chris Anderson*
5. **Explain the new with the familiar.** Use their words, analogies and examples. — *Chris Anderson*
6. **Write it simple.** Short sentences, no jargon. If you'd never say it out loud, cut it. — *MicFront*
7. **Group it in threes.** Three points are easy to follow and easy to remember. — *Carmine Gallo*
8. **Keep it short.** Aim for under 18 minutes; less content gets remembered more. — *Carmine Gallo*
9. **Plan one moment they'll retell.** A prop, a stat, a demo. Bill Gates released mosquitoes at TED. — *Carmine Gallo*

## Opening

10. **Walk on, breathe, then speak.** Don't talk while walking out. Find your spot, pause, begin. — *Simon Sinek*
11. **Open with a promise.** Tell them what they'll know at the end that they don't know now. — *Patrick Winston (MIT)*
12. **Open with a hook, not a joke.** Use a story, a question or a striking fact; save jokes until they know you. — *MicFront, Patrick Winston*

## Your voice

13. **Play your voice like an instrument.** Vary your speed, volume, pitch and pauses; monotone loses rooms. — *Vinh Giang (@askvinh)*
14. **Slow down for what matters.** Move through context quickly, then slow right down on the key line. — *Vinh Giang*
15. **End sentences on a lower pitch.** A downward finish sounds certain; a rising one sounds like a question. — *Vinh Giang*
16. **Slow and lower your pace overall.** It reads as calm, grounded and in control. — *Jefferson Fisher*
17. **Pause instead of "um".** Silence replaces filler words and makes you look sure. — *Matt Abrahams, Vinh Giang*
18. **Pause after your big lines.** Give people time to take it in; silence also pulls attention back. — *Vinh Giang, MicFront*
19. **Warm up your voice.** Big sigh, lip trills ("brrr"), "ba-ba-ba", "la-la-la" before you go on. — *Julian Treasure*
20. **Speak with HAIL.** Honesty, authenticity, integrity, love: people listen to people they trust. — *Julian Treasure*

## Your body

21. **One person, one thought.** Hold one person's eyes for a full sentence, then move on. Don't scan the room. — *Simon Sinek*
22. **Use your hands.** The most-viewed TED speakers used almost twice as many gestures as the least-viewed. — *Vanessa Van Edwards*
23. **Smile when you can.** More smiling tracked with more views, even on serious topics. — *Vanessa Van Edwards*
24. **Stand tall, move with purpose.** Good posture signals confidence; walk to make a point, not to pace. — *MicFront, Matt Abrahams*

## While you speak

25. **Use What → So what → Now what.** The thing, why it matters, the next step. It works for any answer. — *Matt Abrahams*
26. **Say your key idea three times.** People drift, and repeating it brings them back. — *Patrick Winston*
27. **Signpost with numbers and questions.** "Point two…" lets anyone who drifted catch back up. — *Patrick Winston*
28. **Tell a story, not a list.** Stories are what people remember and repeat. — *MicFront*
29. **Contrast what is with what could be.** Go back and forth to build tension, then show the way forward. — *Nancy Duarte*

## Nerves

30. **Say "I'm excited", not "calm down".** People who reframed nerves as excitement gave more persuasive, confident speeches. — *Alison Wood Brooks (Harvard)*
31. **Breathe out longer than you breathe in.** Slow exhales calm your body fastest. — *Matt Abrahams*
32. **Notice the nerves, don't fight them.** Anxiety is normal. Name it and let it sit beside you. — *Matt Abrahams*
33. **Loosen up with a tongue twister.** It warms your mouth and shakes off the tension. — *Matt Abrahams*

## Closing

34. **End on a line they'll remember.** Restate your key message in one strong sentence. — *MicFront*
35. **Don't end on "thank you" or "any questions?"** End on what they now have. That's your last word. — *Patrick Winston*

## Practice

36. **Record and review.** Film a 5-minute unscripted talk. Watch it muted (body), listen without the picture (voice), then transcribe it (filler words). Practise until it's muscle memory. — *Vinh Giang, MicFront*
